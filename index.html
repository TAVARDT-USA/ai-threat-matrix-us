What is the AI Threat Matrix?

The TAVARDT AI Threat Matrix is an interactive technical platform that maps and correlates adversarial attack vectors according to the OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Models, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, and the MITRE ATLAS matrix. The platform enables organizations to diagnose their AI security posture and export tailored hardening blueprints.

Audited Risk Vectors & Defensive Boundaries

Prompt Injection & Jailbreaks (LLM01)

Adversarial manipulation of inputs to bypass operational constraints, safety policies, and domain guardrails.

Insecure Output Handling (LLM02)

Unsanitized model outputs directly consumed by web browsers or backend systems enabling XSS and command injection.

Training Data Poisoning (LLM03)

Compromising pre-training or fine-tuning datasets to introduce hidden backdoors or degrade model decision integrity.

Model Denial of Service (LLM04)

Crafted high-overhead prompts that exhaust context windows, compute allocations, and backend throughput.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) & Technical Guidelines

What is the TAVARDT AI Threat Matrix?
It is an enterprise security diagnostic platform mapping generative AI risk vectors into actionable remediation blueprints.
How do OWASP Top 10 for LLMs and MITRE ATLAS align?
OWASP categorizes high-impact architectural software flaws, while MITRE ATLAS maps empirical adversary tactics and operational techniques.
What are the most critical threats facing autonomous AI agents?
The most critical threats include Insecure Agency (unauthorized tool execution), memory state corruption, and indirect injection via retrieved context.
How do you measure risk posture in enterprise AI systems?
By evaluating perimeter boundaries, backend isolation protocols, tool execution limits, and multi-layered guardrail resilience.
